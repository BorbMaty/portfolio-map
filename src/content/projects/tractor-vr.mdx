## Problem

A tractor simulator is only convincing if the machine behaves like one: it weighs 7.5 t, its wheels slip in mud, and the
plough, the mower and the trailer change how it drives. I wanted all of that to run standalone on a Meta Quest 2, where a
mobile GPU has 13.9 ms per frame at 72 Hz for the physics, a detailed cab and a 2 × 2 km landscape.

## What I built

You drive a John Deere 6195M from the cab, on a farm near Reghin, by day, at dusk or at night. The cab works: you grab the
steering wheel, the speed lever and the hydraulic levers, press the console switches with a fingertip (or with hand
tracking), and open the doors. Four implements hang on a working three-point hitch and front linkage: a plough that leaves
furrows, a front mower that really cuts the meadow, a 900 kg front weight and a tipping trailer.

Free roam lets you explore. The tutorial teaches everything in nine self-checking lessons, each rated with one to five
stars, from driving off to ploughing.

## Architecture

One Unity 6 project (URP, OpenXR) builds three versions: Quest standalone, PC VR streamed over Link, and a PC desktop
version for a monitor. Two quality levels separate them: the PC level adds full-detail meshes and real shadows.

Every environment asset, from the barn to the thousand trees, is modelled by a Python script in Blender and exported in a
full (PC) and a light (Quest) variant, so the Quest version is rebuilt from the same source instead of hand-simplified. A
built-in benchmark drives the same 60 s route in every version and writes its results to the repository.

## Physics

The tractor is a 7.5 t rigidbody on four raycast wheels, simulated at 90 Hz. Each tyre has a friction circle, so braking,
steering and pulling share the same grip, and the ground decides how much grip there is: rolling resistance goes from
0.015 on asphalt to 0.15 in mud. The drivetrain has 4WD, a differential lock and a 20F/20R gearbox, automatic or manual,
driven by a torque curve.

Every formula is written down, with its symbols and numbers, in the [physics document](https://github.com/BorbMaty/TractorVR/blob/main/docs/PHYSICS.md).
An automatic driving test runs after every change to the vehicle; its measured curves sit in the same document, next to
the ones the formulas predict.

## Performance

On the Quest 2, the full world (farm, fields, about 1000 trees, real skies) runs at 70.9 fps on average at 72 Hz; 1.4 % of
the frames go over budget, almost all of them in the cab view. The GPU needs 12.0 ms of the 13.9 ms frame. I measured
every new part of the environment on the headset before keeping it: the landscape costs 1.2–1.5 ms wherever it is in view.

## What I learned

Measure on the target device, early and often. The Quest's frame budget decided more of the design than any feature list:
the light asset variants and the density of the meadow grass came from benchmark runs. Generating
assets by script made that affordable, because a change to one script updates both versions at once.
